# Smart Home Organization Robot — Speech Script

**Target duration:** about 4 minutes 35 seconds, leaving time for pauses and slide changes.  
**Language:** simple spoken English. Do not read the slide word for word.  
**Team:** four speakers.

---

## Speaker 1 — Slides 1–2 · about 1:05

### Slide 1 — *Lost Keys* · 0:00–0:20

*[Act as if you are about to leave. Look for your keys briefly.]*

> Oh no — where are my keys?
>
> Have you ever been late because you could not find something small?
>
> It may be your keys, your earphones, or your charging cable.
>
> The object is usually not really lost. It is somewhere in the room. But we do not know where.

### Slide 2 — *The Recurring Problem* · 0:20–1:05

> This happens often in student life.
>
> In a dorm room or a shared apartment, small objects move all the time. We put our keys on the desk one day, on the bed the next day, and then forget.
>
> Looking for them costs time. It can also create stress, especially when we are already in a hurry.
>
> So we asked: why does this small problem keep coming back?

*[Turn to Speaker 2.]*

---

## Speaker 2 — Slide 3 · about 0:45

### Slide 3 — *Why It Keeps Happening* · 1:05–1:50

> We used the Five Whys to look beyond the first problem.
>
> First, people cannot find things because they do not remember where they put them.
>
> Why not? Because small objects move often and do not always have a fixed place.
>
> Returning every object to the same place also takes effort. And doing this every day is difficult to maintain.
>
> So our working root cause is simple: object locations constantly change, but they are not continuously tracked or managed.
>
> This gave us our question: how might we help people easily find and organize everyday objects at home?

*[Turn to Speaker 3.]*

---

## Speaker 3 — Slides 4–5 · about 1:30

### Slide 4 — *We Explored Alternatives* · 1:50–2:30

> We did not start with a robot.
>
> We explored several ideas together: storage boxes, tracking tags, apps, cameras, and a robot.
>
> Each idea has value. Storage is simple. Tags can help find one item. Apps and cameras can record information.
>
> But most of these solutions are passive. They can store, record, or locate. They cannot interact with the physical room.
>
> That is why we selected an embodied robot as our direction.

### Slide 5 — *The Robot Experience* · 2:30–3:20

> Our idea is a Smart Home Organization Robot.
>
> We explain it with four simple actions: see, remember, find, and organize.
>
> First, it sees selected everyday objects in the room.
>
> Then it remembers their recent locations.
>
> When the user needs an item, it helps find it.
>
> Finally, when conditions are suitable, it can support a limited return-to-place action.
>
> The important point is not the technology itself. The important point is making daily life easier.

*[Turn to Speaker 4.]*

---

## Speaker 4 — Slides 6–7 · about 1:15

### Slide 6 — *A Believable First Version* · 3:20–4:00

> We also want to keep the first version believable.
>
> Our MVP is not a robot that can organize everything in every home.
>
> It focuses on one dorm room or shared apartment, and a small group of objects: keys, earphones, and charging cables.
>
> Its first job is to remember locations and help users find these objects. Limited organizing can come later, only when it is safe and suitable.

### Slide 7 — *Value and Closing* · 4:00–4:35

> Our first target users are students living in dormitories or shared apartments.
>
> The value is clear: save time, reduce stress, and make everyday life easier.
>
> Our starting business model is simple: hardware sale for a focused home-organization companion.
>
> Next time you cannot find your keys, do not search the whole room.
>
> Just ask your robot.
>
> Thank you.

---

## Rehearsal Notes

- Keep the opening role-play under 20 seconds.
- Speak slightly slower than normal; short pauses make the English clearer.
- Do not add unsupported market size, price, user-survey, or technical-performance claims.
- If the team is too slow, shorten the Speaker 3 alternative explanations first; do not remove the final MVP boundary or closing line.
